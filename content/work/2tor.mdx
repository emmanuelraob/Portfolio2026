## Problem

Tutoring in Costa Rica runs on word of mouth. Parents struggle to find tutors they trust, and good tutors struggle to find steady students.

## Research questions

How do parents decide whom to trust with their children's learning? What would make tutors commit to a brand-new platform?

## Process

<Figure caption="From 18 interviews to 2 rounds of usability testing. Recreated diagram.">
  <ResearchProcess />
</Figure>

1. **Discovery:** 18 in-depth interviews with parents and tutors, with informed consent under university ethics guidelines.
2. **Synthesis:** JTBD affinity maps, 2 personas (a parent and a tutor), journey maps and value proposition canvases with participant quotes.
3. **Design:** wireframes, then high-fidelity interactive prototypes for the parent and tutor flows.
4. **Testing:** usability testing with 30 participants across 2 iterations, with scripts aligned to the prototype screens.

## Key findings

- **Fear comes first.** Parents' main barrier was leaving their child with an unknown tutor.
- **Not everyone is digital.** Many parents distrusted online platforms and didn't want to pay online, and some had low digital literacy.
- **Virtual isn't enough for young kids.** Parents distrusted fully virtual sessions for young children.
- **Tutors need students from day one.** Marketplace liquidity was the main risk before launch.

## Impact

The research changed the product's scope. We moved from a virtual-only service for private-school families to partnering directly with schools, so parents meet the platform through an institution they already trust. We also redefined the user profiles, added in-person sessions and simplified onboarding.

<Figure caption="How the research changed the product's scope. Recreated diagram.">

Usability testing showed that the first stages of the flow decide whether parents trust the platform; the rest of the flow tested well. The product is now in development.
